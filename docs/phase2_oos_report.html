Bottom line

The frozen bullish windows rose in the unseen period, but no more than randomly timed windows of the same length did in the same bull market (placebo p = 0.36). The bearish and drawdown hypotheses did not identify unusually weak periods. With only 3–4 observations per window, none of this is statistical proof either way.

Mid-April (Apr 16 → Apr 29) and mid-November held up best: they went the right way in every OOS year and beat both baselines. They are the hypotheses worth tracking further. Only 5 of 12 monthly rules were Supportive or Partially Supportive. The mid-year weakness lean (May, June, August) reversed, and January was up in all four years.

Executive summary

  1. Did the 2003–2022 tendencies persist after Jan 1, 2023? Only weakly. Of the 8 primary hypotheses, 4 are Supportive, 1 Partially Supportive, 3 Inconclusive and 0 Contradicted. Across all 132 rules, though, only 52% beat the OOS drift in their original direction, close to the 50% a coin flip gives (p = 0.33). The classification counts were 36 Supportive, 24 Partial, 25 Inconclusive and 47 Contradicted. The in-sample ranking had only a faint relationship with OOS results (Spearman +0.15, nominal one-sided p = 0.041). That p-value is overstated because overlapping windows are not independent.
  2. Strongest support. Apr 16 → Apr 29 was up 4 of 4 years, averaging +1.66% against +1.42% in-sample. It beat the OOS drift by +0.90 pp and is the only hypothesis Supportive on every scorecard line. Nov 16 → Dec 7 was up 3 of 3 (+2.20%), and Nov 16 → Dec 29 3 of 3 (+2.84%); these overlap and are effectively one mid-November effect. Jul 1 → Jul 22 was up 3 of 4, averaging +1.48%.
  3. Which weakened? Jul 1 → Jul 22 kept 56% of its historical edge, and 2026 was flat. Jul 1 → Jul 29 faded to neutral (ρ +0.09) and lagged the OOS drift. Mar 16 → Apr 27 kept its average (+3.11%) only because of two big years; 2024 was flat and 2025 lost -1.95%.
  4. Which failed or reversed? No primary hypothesis met the pre-registered Contradicted definition. Apr 16 → Apr 22 (5 td) effectively vanished: mean +0.04%, and only 1 of 4 years beat its baseline. Sep 16 → Sep 29 (bearish) fell in only 1 of 4 years, averaging +0.04%. It did lag the OOS drift, but it was not a weak period in absolute terms. Among monthly rules, January (bearish lean in-sample) rose every year, and the May, June and August weakness leans reversed.
  5. Did bullish windows beat random holding periods? Not convincingly. The 26 bullish occurrences averaged +1.68%, which is +0.36 pp above same-length random windows in the OOS period, and 65% beat drift. The calendar-scramble placebo gives p = 0.36, so a result this good is common by chance.
  6. Did bearish windows identify weak or high-drawdown periods? No, not as a group. In the 12 pre-identified drawdown windows, OOS 3%+ drawdowns occurred in 22% of 10-day and 35% of 20-day occurrences, against unconditional rates of 18% and 34% (p = 0.41 and 0.55). The exception is late February through March, where 3 of 4 years again had 3%+ drawdowns. The June, January and May windows were calm.
  7. Were OOS magnitudes consistent with the originals? Against the frozen in-sample baselines, the primaries kept 74% of their historical edge overall. But 2023–2026 was a strong market: random 20-day windows averaged +1.51%, against +0.69% in 2003–2022. That flatters every bullish rule. Measured against the OOS drift, the remaining edge is small.
  8. How much confidence? Little. Each window has N = 3–4. A 4-of-4 record has a 95% exact confidence interval of 40%–100% for the true hit rate. The 95% CI for the mean OOS return includes zero for 7 of the 8 primaries; Nov 16 → Dec 7 is the exception. These results can be consistent with a hypothesis; they cannot prove it.
  9. Proceed to a formal walk-forward trading-strategy study? Not yet. Nothing has survived a multiple-testing correction in-sample, and the OOS set as a whole did not beat random timing. A strategy built on this evidence would mostly be trading noise. A better next step is a second, independent holdout: run the same frozen rules on pre-2003 S&P 500 data (for example 1950–2002), which these rules have also never seen. Then keep scoring them each year going forward.
  10. Which deserve additional testing? Apr16+10td and the mid-November pair (Nov16+15td / Nov16+30td), as already frozen. Jul01+15td is a lower priority. These are the same rule IDs as before; nothing was re-tuned.

definitions:
  d: "+1 for a bullish rule, -1 for a bearish rule (frozen designation)"
  r: "simple price return, entry close -> exit close (same as Phase 1)"

  e: "OOS excess = mean(r) - base_IS"
  E_IS: "frozen in-sample mean excess return"
  rho: "e / E_IS  (share of the historical edge that reappeared; >0 = same direction)"
  hit_excess: "share of OOS occurrences with d*(r - base_IS) > 0"
  win_raw: "share of OOS occurrences with r > 0 (reported, but not used to classify — drift makes it easy for bullish rules)"

effect_direction:
  # if |E_IS| < 0.0025 the ratio is unstable; use absolute thresholds on d*e instead (in brackets)
  same_direction:         "rho >= 0.75            [d*e >= +0.25%]"
  weaker_same_direction:  "0.25 <= rho < 0.75     [n/a]"
  neutral:                "-0.25 < rho < 0.25     [|e| < 0.25%]"
  opposite_direction:     "rho <= -0.25           [d*e <= -0.25%]"

validation_classification:
  INCONCLUSIVE_small_n: "N < 3 completed OOS occurrences"
  SUPPORTIVE: "N >= 3 AND rho >= 0.50 AND hit_excess >= 2/3"
  PARTIALLY_SUPPORTIVE: "N >= 3 AND rho >= 0.25 AND hit_excess >= 0.50, and not SUPPORTIVE"
  CONTRADICTED: "N >= 3 AND rho <= -0.25 AND hit_excess <= 0.50"
  INCONCLUSIVE: "everything else"
  note: "With N = 3-4, SUPPORTIVE means 'consistent with the hypothesis', never 'statistically proven'. Proof would need the aggregate tests below."

scorecard_components:   # for the 8 primary hypotheses
  direction_match: "Supportive if rho >= 0.25; Partially Supportive if 0 < rho < 0.25; Contradicted if rho <= -0.25; else Inconclusive"
  win_rate_consistency: "compare OOS hit_excess with IS hit rate in the designated direction: Supportive if within 25 pp or higher; Partially if 25-50 pp lower; Contradicted if > 50 pp lower"
  return_consistency: "Supportive if rho >= 0.5; Partially if 0.25 <= rho < 0.5; Contradicted if rho <= -0.25; else Inconclusive"
  drawdown_consistency: "Bullish: Supportive if OOS avg max-DD is no worse than IS avg max-DD + 1 pp; Partially if within +3 pp; else Contradicted. Bearish: Supportive if OOS avg max-DD is at least as deep as IS avg; Partially if within 1 pp shallower; else Contradicted"

  overall: "= the rule's validation_classification above"

  primary: "frozen in-sample same-length mean (base_IS), per the frozen protocol"
  secondary: "OOS unconditional mean of ALL overlapping same-length windows 2023-01-03..hard_stop (contemporaneous drift)"
  placebo: "calendar scramble on OOS data: for each completed occurrence-year, start at a uniformly random OOS trading day; 10,000 draws; percentile of the observed mean return within the placebo distribution. Context only — never used to create or move rules."
  seed: 20230101

  Q4_direction: "counts of same / weaker / neutral / opposite across the 8 and across all 132; binomial test that the share of 132 rules with d*e > 0 exceeds 50%."
  Q5_random: "placebo percentile of each primary rule and of the pooled bullish mean."

  • Flagged prices: 2025-04-03 (daily move -4.84%), 2025-04-04 (daily move -5.97%), 2025-04-09 (daily move +9.52%). These are the real April-2025 tariff-shock moves, kept as-is. No bar had high < low or a close outside its range.

  • Point-in-time: every trade is evaluated through MarketData.as_of(exit_date), the unchanged Phase-1 class. Requesting a later bar raises LookAheadError. The 11 Phase-1 tests and 11 new Phase-2 tests all pass (22/22). The new tests cover pre-2023 rejection, post-hard-stop rejection, the frozen-file hash, the criteria lock, and the exclusion of incomplete occurrences.
  • Completeness: an occurrence counts only if its exit close is on or before 2026-10-02. Windows still open on that date (for example Sep16+20td and Oct01+10td in 2026) and October 2026 are listed as incomplete and excluded from all statistics. Windows that had not started yet (mid-November and December 2026) are listed as not started.

Aggregate results

  1. Did the bullish hypotheses collectively beat chance? No. Pooled excess over the frozen baseline was +1.07 pp per occurrence, but over the OOS drift only +0.36 pp. The calendar-scramble placebo with common yearly shifts gives p = 0.36.
  2. Did bearish and drawdown hypotheses flag elevated downside? No, as a group (p = 0.41 and 0.55). Sep 16 → Sep 29 averaged +0.04% with 1 of 4 years down. Only the late-February and March windows repeated.
  3. Similar magnitude? Against the frozen baselines, pooled ρ = 0.74. Against contemporaneous drift the effects shrink to near zero, and the 2023–26 bull market accounts for most of the difference.
  4. Weaken, persist, strengthen or reverse? Primaries: 4 same direction, 3 neutral, 1 weaker same direction. All 132 rules: 53 opposite direction, 47 same direction, 18 neutral, 14 weaker same direction.
  5. Better than randomly timed windows? Placebo percentiles for the 8 primaries ranged from 35th to 76th. None reached the 95th, and the pooled test was not significant.
